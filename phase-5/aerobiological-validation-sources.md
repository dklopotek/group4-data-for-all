## Source 1 — XAC Barcelona Station (Hirst volumetric trap, 1994–2026)

**Responsible:** Prof. Jordina Belmonte, Laboratori d'Anàlisis Palinològiques, UAB-ICTA
**Analysts:** David Navarro, Rut Puigdemunt (formerly Mercè Vendrell, Anna Guàrdia, Elena Gabarra)
**Medical collaborator:** Catalan Society of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (SCAIC)
**Sensor:** Hirst (Lanzoni) — volumetric active trap; gold standard in aerobiology; counts grains/m³ air
**Data period:** 1994–2026 (continuous, 32 years)
**Coordinates:** 41°23'37"N, 02°09'54"E | Altitude: 67 m a.s.l., 25 m above ground
**Contact:** jordina.belmonte@uab.cat | Tel: 935812040
**URL:** https://lap.uab.cat/aerobiologia/

### Rubric scoring (adapted from Phase-2 rubric)

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance | 3 | Peer-reviewed lab, SCAIC-affiliated, cited in 100+ publications since 1983 |
| Resolution | 1 | Single point station — temporal resolution excellent, spatial resolution = 0 (one trap covers all Barcelona) |
| Coverage | 3 | 32 continuous years (1994–2026); active today |
| License | 2 | Published outputs freely accessible; raw time-series data requires request (CC-BY-NC) |
| Bias | 2 | Siting documented; urban background station, not street-level; known ~15–30 km catchment radius |
| **TOTAL** | **11/15** | |

### What it provides

- **30-year aggregate P/m³ chart**: primary peak March–April reaching **~750 P/m³ mean,
  ~2250 P/m³ historic maximum** for total pollen; Platanus dominates the March–April window
- **Real-time weekly forecast**: https://aerobiologia.cat — updated weekly for all 9 XAC stations
- **Current reading (week of 15–21 June 2026):** Platanus = **level 1** (season confirmed over)

### Key finding for validation

> Platanus pollen season is confined to **3–5 weeks in March–April**, confirmed by 32 years of
> continuous Hirst trap measurement. This validates the seasonal framing of our static exposure map.

### Critical limitation

Single trap = **zero spatial resolution within Barcelona**. The XAC station validates the
*temporal* and *species* claim but cannot rank grid cells spatially. This gap is an honest
limitation to state in the evaluation report.

## Source 2 — XAC Barcelona Station (Cour passive trap, 1983–1994)

**Responsible:** Prof. Jordina Belmonte, UAB
**Sensor:** Cour passive trap — different methodology from Hirst; data not directly comparable
**Data period:** 1983–1985, 1988–1994 (with gaps)
**Coordinates:** 41°24'N, 02°09'E | Altitude: 93 m a.s.l., 25 m above ground
**URL:** https://lap.uab.cat/aerobiologia/

### What it provides

Historical aerobiological record extending the Barcelona series back to **1983** — over 40 years
of combined monitoring when paired with Source 1. The Cour method cannot be merged with Hirst
data without calibration (different units), but it establishes that **Platanus was already a
dominant allergen in Barcelona in the 1980s**.

### Use in project

Cite as evidence that the allergen burden is a **structural, long-standing urban problem**, not a
recent trend — reinforcing the capital-planning framing. Do not use Cour numbers alongside Hirst
numbers without noting the methodological gap.

## Source 3 — Barcelona Pollen Calendar (Belmonte et al., UAB/SCAIC/PIA)

**Full citation:**
> Belmonte, J., Roure, J.M., Botey, J., Cadahía, A. & Eseverri, J.L. *Calendari pol·línic de
> Barcelona*. Laboratori d'Anàlisis Palinològiques, UAB; Unitat d'Al·lèrgia, Hospital Universitari
> Vall d'Hebrón; Unitat d'Al·lergologia Pediàtrica, Hospital Universitari Materno-Infantil Vall
> d'Hebrón. PIA/XAC, UAB. [PDF available: `assets/aerobiology/cal_pol_barcelona_ca.pdf`]

**Publisher:** PIA (Punt d'Informació Aerobiològica) / XAC / UAB

### What it provides

Official seasonal risk classification for **36 pollen taxa** in Barcelona, on a 4-level scale:

| Level | Concentration | Allergy risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Baix (Low) | Poc important | Rares |
| 2. Mig (Medium) | Mitjanes | Risc possible |
| 3. Alt (High) | Molt elevades | Risc important |
| 4. Molt alt (Very high) | Màximes | Risc màxim |

**Platanus (Plàtan d'ombra) reading:**
- Peak: **March–April, level 3–4** (maximum risk tier)
- Only a handful of species reach level 4 in Barcelona; Platanus is among them

**Key observation from the calendar:**
> "Prop de les plantes els nivells de pol·len poden ser més elevats que els representats en els
> gràfics." — Near the source plants, levels can be higher than shown. This directly supports the
> rationale for street-level spatial priority mapping.

### Presentation graph

The calendar chart itself (PDF page 1, Platanus row) is a ready-made figure for the presentation.
Annotate the Platanus row and the March–April window.

## Source 4 — 30-Year Aggregate Pollen Chart (XAC Barcelona, 1994–2025)

**Source:** aerobiologia.cat, XAC Barcelona station historical viewer
**Data:** P/m³ daily readings aggregated across 1994–2025; shows min/max envelope (black) and
current year (red fill); orange vertical line = current date

### Quantified findings

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Primary peak window | March–April |
| Mean peak concentration (Platanus-dominated) | ~750 P/m³ |
| Historic maximum spike | ~2250 P/m³ |
| Secondary peak (grasses, POACEAE) | May–June, ~250 P/m³ mean |
| June 14, 2026 reading | ~0 P/m³ (season over) |

### What this proves

1. **Platanus is the dominant allergen event of the year** — the March–April spike dwarfs all other
   months including the grass season
2. **Inter-annual variability is high** — historic max is ~3× the mean; a static priority map
   represents a typical-year risk, not worst-case. State this as a model scope boundary.
3. **Seasonal framing confirmed in real time** — the orange vertical line (June 14, 2026) falls
   after the peak and shows near-zero concentrations, confirming the season is March–April only

### Presentation graph

The chart image (screenshot from aerobiologia.cat) with annotations on: (1) primary peak,
(2) mean vs. max envelope, (3) today's vertical line, (4) secondary grass peak for contrast.

## Source 5 — Fitxa Botànica: Plàtan d'ombra (SCAIC/PIA, 2022)

**Full citation:**
> Belmonte, J. et al. *Fitxa Botànica: Plàtan d'ombra (Platanus x hybrida)*. SCAIC / PIA /
> UAB-ICTA-BABVE, 2022. [PDF: `assets/aerobiology/Fitxa_Botanica_SCAIC-PIA_2022_Platan_d_ombra.pdf`]

**Publisher:** SCAIC (Catalan Society of Allergy and Clinical Immunology) + PIA/XAC + UAB

### Botanical facts relevant to the model

- Species: *Platanus x hybrida* (hybrid of *P. orientalis* × *P. occidentalis*)
- Pollination: **anemophilous** (wind-pollinated) — wind is the dispersal mechanism
- Season: **3–5 weeks, spring only**, very sudden onset
- Urban ubiquity: "planta habitual a l'entorn urbà" — streets, avenues, parks, gardens

### Medical evidence (direct quotes)

> "El pol·len de plàtan és responsable d'un **gran nombre d'al·lèrgies** a l'inici de la
> primavera i aquestes poden començar de manera **molt brusca** perquè la pol·linització és molt
> sobtada."
>
> Translation: *Platanus pollen is responsible for a large number of allergies at the start of
> spring, and these can begin very suddenly because pollination is very abrupt.*

**Clinical effects documented:**
- Rhinitis, conjunctivitis (upper airways) — most exposed people
- Asthma triggering from sub-pollen microparticles (tapetal debris) reaching lower airways
- Cross-reactions with peach, apple, hazelnut, walnut, peanut
- Coincident fruit dispersal (hairy achenes) causes additional irritation independent of allergy

### Amplifying factors documented in the Fitxa

The following environmental factors **raise concentrations** — each is a co-factor with our model:

| Factor | Relevance to our model |
|---|---|
| Wind ("dies amb vent") | Justifies Phase 6 wind dispersion layer |
| NO2 / air pollution ("contaminació ambiental") | Directly validates CALIOPE-Urban NO2 co-factor layer |
| Dry, sunny days | Confirms seasonal (spring) framing |
| Midday hours (peak concentration) | Supports pedestrian exposure focus |
| Proximity to source plants | Validates street-level spatial ranking rationale |

### Multi-station comparison (from Fitxa Fig. 2)

The Fitxa includes a Catalonia-wide comparative calendar across 9 XAC stations:

| Station | Peak timing | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Vielha (mountain) | February–March, short | Low–Medium |
| Planes de Son | February–March | Low |
| Girona | March–April | Medium–High |
| Manresa | March–April | Medium–High |
| Lleida | March–April | Medium–High |
| **Bellaterra** | **March–April** | **High** |
| **Barcelona** | **March–April** | **High (level 3–4)** |
| Tarragona | March–April | Medium |
| Roquetes (Tortosa) | March–April | Medium |

Barcelona is among the highest-intensity stations in Catalonia — consistent with its dense
urban Platanus street-tree cover.

## Source 8 — Infrared SDK / infrared.city (Phase 6 Wind Analysis)

> **Status: Phase 6 recommendation only. NOT part of the current frozen pipeline.**
> Logged here for future implementation planning.

**SDK:** `infrared-sdk` (Python) — `pip install infrared-sdk`
**Platform:** infrared.city (urban microclimate simulation)
**Skill available:** `/infrared` in this project environment

### What it would add

Infrared runs urban wind simulations including:
- **PWC (Pedestrian Wind Comfort)** — Lawson criteria at street level
- **Wind speed and direction maps** at building resolution
- **Solar radiation** and thermal comfort (UTCI)

For the allergen project, a March–April wind dispersion layer would show:
- Which grid cells are **downwind** of high-density Platanus streets during peak season
- How wind corridors in the Eixample grid channel pollen into accumulation zones
- Whether grid cells with moderate tree counts but high wind exposure rank higher than
  cells with dense trees but wind shelter

### Why it matters

The Fitxa Botànica (Source 5) explicitly names wind as a concentration amplifier. Barcelona's
sea breeze pattern during spring (typically SW–NE) combined with the Eixample grid creates
predictable pollen corridors. This would upgrade the model from a **static density map** to a
**dynamic exposure corridor map**.

### Implementation sketch (Phase 6)

1. Define study polygon: Barcelona municipality boundary
2. Input: building geometry (OSM) + March–April prevailing wind direction (Meteo.cat/AEMET)
3. Run PWC simulation via Infrared SDK
4. Extract wind speed/direction at 400m grid centroids
5. Multiply tree-density score by wind amplification factor per cell
6. Re-rank priority grid

### Data needed

- Barcelona building footprints: OSM (already in pipeline)
- March–April wind roses: Meteo.cat Barcelona station (publicly available)
- Infrared API key: register at infrared.city
