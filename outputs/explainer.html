Barrier-reduction priority map · 400 m grid · 2026

Mature trees cost less than dead saplings.

Here are fifteen places in Barcelona where the next ones will live — and where a euro of de-paving, cooling, or planting does the most measurable work.

For: Espais Verds i Biodiversitat — capital planning Output: shortlist + intervention type per zone Scope: Barcelona municipal boundary
The problem

The same blocks keep losing the same trees.

Barcelona plants thousands of street trees a year. In the hottest, most sealed parts of the city, they do not make it past their third summer. The replacement order is signed; the sapling is replaced; the next sapling fails in the same pit. The cost is not theoretical: in disturbed urban substrate, a failed-and-replaced young tree costs roughly three to five times the original planting once removal, soil repair, and re-establishment are included.

The question planners have not yet been handed a defensible answer to is not where should we plant. It is where will what we plant actually root and live past year three.

Soil life is municipal plumbing. Invisible, easy to break with surface work, expensive to replace — and the reason new tenants do or do not survive their first summer.

Four barriers, mapped

What the model measures.

The composite barrier score per 400 m cell stacks four sub-scores. Three are physically measurable from above-ground sources; the fourth is a flag we have to be honest about.

Sealed surface

Asphalt and concrete cap. Roots cannot expand, water cannot infiltrate. Source: Copernicus Urban Atlas (10 m).

Heat anomaly

Surface-temperature offset from the city median in summer. Above +5 °C, sapling stress is acute. Source: Landsat 8/9 thermal.

Low canopy

NDVI from cloud-masked summer composite. Thin canopy means thin shade and thin root-zone moisture buffer. Source: Sentinel-2 L2A.

Host mismatch — flag, not gradient

Barcelona's street trees are mostly arbuscular-mycorrhizal hosts. AM fungi produce no fruiting bodies, so for ~95 % of cells this sub-score is "expected-but-unconfirmable." We treat it as a flag.

Robustness proof

Three weightings. The same fifteen zones.

Before trusting a ranking, you should know whether it survives the analyst tweaking the weights. We re-ran the composite with three different weighting philosophies — equal-weight, sealed-surface-priority, and heat-priority — and compared the top-15 lists.

A high Jaccard overlap across schemes means the ranking is not a knob we tuned to get a preferred answer. The barriers stack physically in these zones; no weighting choice changes that. Honesty note: the current chart shows Jaccard ≈ 1.0; part of that is genuine signal and part is a known data artefact in the sealed-surface raster being repaired. Expected post-fix Jaccard is 0.7–0.9 — still a robustness result, just an honest one.

The connectivity story

Where the priority zones cluster — and where they don't.

The priority shortlist is not just fifteen scattered points. Several zones sit within 500 m of each other and form contiguous neighbourhoods of high-leverage cells, particularly across Sants–Montjuïc and Sant Andreu. Those clusters are where a single Eix Verd alignment can serve more than one priority zone at once.

Caveat: this is a 500 m connectivity neighbourhood — a spatial-adjacency view of the shortlist — not a 2030 forecast and not a measured belowground network. It tells you where co-located capital spend has logistical leverage. It does not tell you that fungi will reconnect.

What this map cannot claim

The honest footer.

Decision-support only. These are the boundaries you should be ready to defend in a council session.

  • Not a measured belowground map. No soil sampling, no DNA, no in-situ measurement. The pipeline maps surface and atmospheric barriers — sealed surface, heat, low canopy — that are observable from above ground.
  • Does not predict mycorrhizal recovery within a planning cycle. Soil-microbial recovery timescales are documented at 5–20+ years. Eixos Verds operates on 3–10-year cycles. The map identifies leverage, not outcomes.
  • Host-mismatch sub-score is informationally near-null for ~95 % of the city. AM fungi produce no fruiting bodies, so AM-dominant zones receive an identical "expected-but-unconfirmable" flag. Treat it as a flag, not a gradient.
  • Highest barrier index ≠ highest intervention leverage in every case. A severely degraded soil may need structural intervention (drainage, decontamination) before de-paving or planting can have effect.
  • Budget-line crosswalk is pending verification. Intervention-type assignments are decision-support suggestions, not confirmed allocations against current Ajuntament documents (open Seam 3).
