The one decision

Where should the city cut plane trees first?

Barcelona has already committed to removing plane trees (Platanus) — they cause a big share of the city's spring pollen allergies. The trees come out over years. The only question we answer:

"Given we're removing planes anyway — in what order should we cut, so each removal relieves the most pollen for the most people?"

User: a capital-planning analyst at Espais Verds (Barcelona city greenery). Not a dashboard-watcher — someone sequencing a multi-year work plan.

priority = Source × People
  • Source = how many big mature plane trees are here (the pollen)
  • People = how many residents live here (who breathes it)
  • Multiply → the spots where cutting helps the most people the most

The deployment gap

A 400m square isn't an instruction.

The shipped map scores 494 squares of a 400m grid. A planner can't act on "intervene in this square" — it's not a real boundary, and the squares are arbitrary.

So tonight I push the product to where action lives:

  • Priority → census sections (1,068 real admin units; also the native grain of the population data, so we drop a fudgy interpolation step)
  • Action → per street: for top sections, the actual mature plane trees on each street, from the city's 40,444-tree inventory

The line we don't cross

Section = a priority claim ✓   (defensible)

Street = a priority claim ✗   (ecological fallacy)

Street output is an inventory + feasibility list ("here are the planes you could cut on this street"), never a "this street ranks #3" score. Enforced by a grep gate.

5 / 9
The honest finding (the interesting bit)

The answer changes with the map's grain. That's MAUP.

At fine grain, a few sections (e.g. Av. Diagonal) hold a massive cluster of mature planes — the top section has 594, the rest of the top-50 average ~63. That spike is so tall that multiplying by population can't change who's #1.

So the ranking quietly collapses to "wherever the most planes are." The same method gives a different answer at a different zoom — the classic Modifiable Areal Unit Problem.

We caught it in our own product. Most projects never test for this. Verdict, stated honestly: 400m map = the evidence that people-weighting helps; section map = the unit a planner acts on. Both shipped, with this caveat written down.
