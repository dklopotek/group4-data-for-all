Phase 1 — Business Understanding

Barcelona is already removing plane trees.
The only question is: in what order?

The standing policy (Pla Director de l'Arbrat 2017–2037):
Cut planes from 27.45% → under 12% of all urban trees.
43,722 specimens today. ~56% will be removed by 2037.
Reason stated by the city: biodiversity + monoculture risk.

Platanus is responsible for ~46% of Barcelona's annual pollen index (Gabarra et al. 2002). Removing a plane also relieves allergy burden — but the city doesn't rank by that. We supply the missing sequencing criterion.

Our decision target

In what spatial order should the city cut planes so that each removal relieves the most pollen-allergen exposure for the people who live there?

User: capital-planning analyst at Espais Verds.
Unit of action: census section → street-level worklist.

Session 5 — Phase 5: Evaluation

Three independent lines of evidence.
One verdict: stop the mycorrhizal claim.

Line 1 — Internal redundancy

R² 0.91
composite explained by sealed surface alone

Effective weight of ecological components: s4_mismatch r = −0.015, PRPI r = +0.18. Sealed surface: r = +0.95. Our "mycorrhizal map" was a paved-surface map in disguise.

Line 2 — Pre-registered external test

p = 0.99
partial-F, biotic block vs GBIF occurrences

1,024 independent fungal occurrences (GBIF). Biotic/host layers added ΔadjR² = −0.02 beyond an abiotic null. Flat null. Design committed before result.

Line 3 — Literature (44 sources)

Weak lever
AM → EM host mismatch, peer-reviewed evidence

Counter-evidence from comparable cities: plane-tree fungal diversity increased with urbanization in Amsterdam — the opposite of what our mechanism assumed.

CRISP-DM verdict: stop / iterate. Not "relabel and ship." A defensible STOP is a result.

Cycle B — Shipped

Allergen-exposure priority

Same tree inventory. New question: where to sequence the removal the city is already doing, for maximum pollen relief.

priority = SOURCE × EXPOSURE
  • SOURCE = plane density × maturity score (min-max std)
  • EXPOSURE = residential population, interpolated (min-max std)
  • Multiplicative — non-compensatory by design (fixes Cycle A's failure)
  • Feasibility = 1 − sealed fraction (annotation, never multiplied in)

Why multiplicative?

A weighted sum (Cycle A) lets one high-variance component dominate regardless of declared weights. A product is non-compensatory: a cell with no planes or no people cannot score high. There are no hidden weights to mis-set.

Phase 6 — Deployment

What we ship — and one honest
negative we found in our own output.

  • Priority map (400 m cell grain) — v1 efficiency + v3 equity, interactive HTML with census-section toggle
  • Census-section ranking (1,068 sections, native grain, no interpolation) — the action-facing product
  • Street-level worklist — top-60 sections, 401 streets, 100% address coverage. Inventory + feasibility; no street priority score (ecological fallacy guardrail)
  • Model card + datasheet + monitoring plan — FAIR-compliant handoff package

The finding we measured in our own output: MAUP

At section grain, one park section (Montjuïc: 594 mature planes, ~2,000 residents) dominates. T1 re-order test fails (Spearman 0.97, J₁₅ = 0.58). Rollup disagreement with cell product: Spearman 0.47.

This is the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem — the answer changes with the map's grain. We measured it rather than hid it, and we ship both grains with the caveat explicit.
