## 1. Executive Summary

For each of the four intervention types the Mycorrhizal Barcelona pipeline recommends — **de-paving**, **cooling**, **planting**, and **species-selection** — this report attaches a defensible EUR cost range, sourced primarily from Ajuntament de Barcelona budget documents (Pla Calor 2025–2035, Trees for Life Master Plan 2017–2037, the BIMSA public-procurement contract for the four Eixample green axes) and triangulated against peer-reviewed European nature-based-solutions cost studies (Cortinovis et al. 2024, Pauleit et al. 2002).

The headline numbers a planner can defend in a budget memo:

| Intervention | Central EUR | Range | Unit | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **De-paving + structural soil + paving rebuild** (Eixos Verds grade) | **600** | 450 – 800 | per m² (incl. VAT) | HIGH |
| **Cooling — shade structures** | **200** | 80 – 450 | per m² | MEDIUM |
| **Cooling — full school climate-shelter retrofit** | **455,000** | 200,000 – 750,000 | per facility | HIGH |
| **Planting — tree only (Mediterranean Europe)** | **800** | 200 – 1,500 | per tree | MEDIUM |
| **Planting — tree + structural soil pit (Eixos Verds grade)** | **3,500** | 1,500 – 6,000 | per tree | MEDIUM |
| **Annual tree maintenance (Barcelona derived)** | **31** | 20 – 50 | per tree per year | HIGH |
| **Species-selection — marginal cost over default** | **50** | 0 – 200 | per tree | LOW |
| **Mycorrhizal inoculation** | **5** | 1 – 25 | per tree | MEDIUM |

The "**mature trees cost less than dead saplings**" framing in the storytelling guidance is quantitatively defensible. With Mediterranean 5-year sapling mortality of 13–46% (Hilbert et al. 2019 IQR) and replacement cost equal to the original planting cost plus removal/soil-repair (~3–5× per the storytelling guidance), the break-even moment for spending an additional EUR 1,000–3,000 on de-paving and structural soil per tree is met whenever marginal mortality reduction exceeds ~20%. In Barcelona's hottest, most-sealed zones — i.e. the 15 zones the pipeline identifies — that condition almost certainly holds.

The full city-wide Eixos Verds programme is estimated to prevent **178 premature deaths per year** (Iungman et al. 2025, Environment International). The full Superblock plan is estimated to prevent **667 premature deaths per year** with an annual economic value of **EUR 1.7 billion** (Mueller et al. 2020, Environment International). Even at the lower confidence bound (EUR 0.6 billion / year, 235 deaths) the implied "value per prevented death" is on the order of EUR 2.5 million — orders of magnitude larger than the per-intervention costs analysed here.

The single highest-value verification step still outstanding is a direct conversation with Parcs i Jardins de Barcelona to obtain the actual per-tree planting unit cost on current 2025–2026 supply contracts. That figure exists in the Ajuntament tendering system but is not publicly disaggregated.

## 2. Per-Intervention Cost Table

### 2.1 De-paving (the dominant intervention for all 15 priority zones)

**Anchor: the BIMSA contract.** The most defensible Barcelona-specific number comes from the public procurement contract for the first four Eixample green axes (UP1 Consell de Cent, UP2 Girona, UP3 Rocafort, UP4 Comte Borrell) plus four plazas (Rocafort, Comte Borrell, Enric Granados, Girona):

> **Contract value: EUR 42,065,451.99 ex-VAT / EUR 50,899,196.90 inc-VAT.** Funded by Fons Next Generation EU. Divided into 10 lots. Contracting authority: Barcelona d'Infraestructures Municipals (BIMSA), Ajuntament de Barcelona.

Source: Diputació de Barcelona CIDO public procurement registry, contract reference 12969663 ([link](https://cido.diba.cat/contractacio/12969663/obres-relatives-als-projectes-dels-nous-eixos-verds-up1-eix-consell-de-cent-up2-eix-girona-up3-eix-rocafort-up4-eix-borrell-i-les-places-rocafort-comte-borrell-enric-granados-i-placa-girona-al-districte-de-leixample-ajuntament-de-barcelona-barcelona-dinfraestructures-municipals-sa-bimsa)). **Confidence: HIGH** — this is a published public-tender award value.

Cross-checked against the Ajuntament's own announcements: total programme budget EUR 52.7M (EUR 47.9–48.2M axes + EUR 4.4–4.5M plazas), with EUR 25M from Next Generation EU funds. The small delta between EUR 50.9M (BIMSA contract) and EUR 52.7M (announced) reflects ancillary costs outside the main works contract.

**Surface area:** The four axes together span 4.65 km of street length and 11.12 hectares (111,200 m²) of axis surface. Architects ACPA and B67 report a project area of **86,000 m²** for the directly transformed footprint. The Ajuntament reports **58,000 m² gained for pedestrians** plus 4 × 2,000 m² main plazas + 31 × 725 m² small squares = ~88,500 m² of total reorganised surface.

**Implied unit cost:** EUR 50.9M inc-VAT / 86,000 m² ≈ **EUR 592/m²** (or EUR 489/m² ex-VAT). Adopting EUR 600/m² as the central planner-facing figure is conservative and inclusive.

**What that EUR 600/m² actually buys.** This is a **composite** figure — it bundles:

- Asphalt removal and disposal
- Sub-base reconstruction
- Structural soil installation (Stockholm-method analogue for new tree pits)
- New permeable paving where applicable
- Drainage and sustainable urban drainage system (SUDS)
- Tree pits and 500 new tree plantings within the four axes
- Urban furniture, lighting, signage
- Mobility reorganisation (traffic calming, kerb works)

A pure-asphalt-removal figure alone — the strict de-paving line — would be lower (~EUR 30–80/m² in European peer-reviewed literature; Cortinovis et al. 2024 cite a median EUR 56/m² for "green spaces" intervention generically). But for a Barcelona planner sizing the next Eix Verd, the composite figure is what their procurement system will produce, and is the correct anchor.

**Per-zone application:** Given the priority-zone grid cell is ~400 m × 400 m = 160,000 m², a full Eix Verd-grade rebuild of 1/4 of that area (the streets within one cell, ~40,000 m²) would cost on the order of **EUR 18–32 million per zone** at central rates. Most planners will adopt a smaller fraction of the cell for active intervention; a more realistic per-zone capital ask might be EUR 3–8M for a partial street-by-street rollout.

**Maintenance.** Press citations of Ajuntament reporting indicate the Eixample green-axes maintenance budget rose by approximately **EUR 1.1M per year** after the four axes opened ([source](https://www.larepublica.cat/minut-a-minut/el-manteniment-dels-eixos-verds-de-leixample-de-barcelona-sha-encarit-prop-d11-milions-deuros-segons-lajuntament/)). Across the ~86,000 m² this is ~EUR 12.8/m²/year. Confidence: MEDIUM (derived from a press citation of an internal Ajuntament budget figure).

**Lifetime.** Structural soil systems are designed for a 30+ year service life if not damaged by utility works. Surface paving 15–25 years.

### 2.2 Cooling

Cooling here means urban-heat-island mitigation that is **not** primarily tree canopy. Tree-canopy cooling is captured under "planting." This category covers misters, fountains, reflective and draining pavements, shade structures, and climate-shelter retrofits of public buildings.

**Anchor 1: Pla Calor 2025–2035.** The headline programme.

> **Total budget: EUR 111.6 million** over 2025–2035 (i.e. average EUR ~11.2M/year). Roughly 50 actions across 7 objectives. Headline targets: +160 ha new green space by 2030 (under Pla Natura), 9 ha shade target, draining/reflective pavements, fountain optimisation, climate shelters in every district, school AC, vulnerable-people support.

Source: Ajuntament press release 26 June 2025 ([link](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/premsa/2025/06/26/pla-calor-lestrategia-de-barcelona-cap-a-la-ciutat-resilient-de-2035/)) and BCNROC repository ([link](https://bcnroc.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/jspui/handle/11703/141698)). Confidence: HIGH.

**Anchor 2: Refugis Climàtics a les Escoles (2019–2022).** The single best Barcelona-specific cost data for cooling interventions, because the works were itemised in a peer-reviewed EU Urban Innovative Actions (UIA) project.

> **Total budget: EUR 5,000,000** (EU UIA / ERDF EUR 4,000,000 + Ajuntament EUR 1,000,000).
> **Outputs across 11 schools:** 1,000 m² of new natural soil (de-paved playground), 2,213 m² of new shade space (pergolas, awnings), 74 trees planted, 26 new water points.

Source: Ajuntament press release Oct 2018 ([link](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/premsa/2018/10/11/la-comissio-europea-concedeix-4me-a-barcelona-en-el-marc-de-les-iniciatives-innovadores-urbanes-per-impulsar-refugis-climatics-a-les-escoles/)) and EU UIA project page. Confidence: HIGH.

**Implied unit costs:**
- Per school full retrofit: **EUR 454,545** (central estimate EUR 455,000)
- Shade structure unit cost (if allocated 50% of programme cost): EUR 2.5M / 2,213 m² ≈ **EUR 1,130/m²** for the composite shade + plumbing + electrical retrofit. Standalone shade unit cost in European NbS literature is lower (EUR 80–450/m²).

**Anchor 3: Pla Clima umbrella (2024–2030).** EUR 1,800,000,000 total / EUR ~360M per year across all climate-emergency lines including Pla Calor, Pla Resiliència, Pla Mobilitat, Pla Transició Energètica, Pla Clima Barris.

Source: Beteve coverage citing Ajuntament strategy launch ([link](https://beteve.cat/politica/pla-clima-barcelona-destina-1800-milions-euros-ciutat-verda-sostenible/)) + EIB press release Mar 2025 confirming Pla Clima envelope ([link](https://www.eib.org/en/press/all/2025-125-el-bei-y-barcelona-firman-un-prestamo-de-175-millones-de-euros-para-financiar-proyectos-de-regeneracion-urbana)). Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.

**For per-zone application:** A "cooling-dominant" intervention in a 400m grid cell — say a climate shelter retrofit plus a small plaza misting installation plus shade pergolas across ~1,000 m² of pedestrian corridor — would size to roughly **EUR 400,000 to 1,200,000 per zone**. A larger cooling footprint with a fountain network and reflective pavement repaving would push to EUR 2–4M.

### 2.3 Planting

This is the hardest intervention to price with Barcelona-specific precision because the Ajuntament publishes aggregate budget figures, not unit prices.

**Anchor 1: Trees for Life Master Plan 2017–2037.**

> **Annual budget: EUR 9.6 million per year**, of which EUR 8.3M operational and EUR 1.3M capital improvements (mainly improved soil and water management).
> **Tree inventory under management: ~310,800 trees**, broken down as 201,600 street trees, 35,700 park trees, and 73,500 woodland trees.
> **Target: increase tree canopy from 25% to 30% over 2017–2037 (5 percentage points in 20 years).**

Source: Ajuntament "Arbres per Viure" Tree Master Plan ([link to ENG PDF](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/ecologiaurbana/sites/default/files/Pla-director-arbrat-barcelona-ENG.pdf)) and Climate-ADAPT EEA case study ([link](https://climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu/en/metadata/case-studies/barcelona-trees-tempering-the-mediterranean-city-climate)). Confidence: HIGH.

**Implied unit cost: maintenance.** EUR 8.3M operational / 310,800 trees = **EUR 26.7 per tree per year**. Allowing for higher attention to street trees relative to woodland, EUR 31/tree/year is a defensible street-tree central estimate.

**Anchor 2: European street-tree establishment survey (Pauleit et al. 2002).** The peer-reviewed European benchmark.

> Street-tree establishment costs reported across 17 European countries: **under EUR 200 to over EUR 1,500 per tree.**

Source: Pauleit, Jones, Garcia-Martin et al. (2002), *Urban Forestry & Urban Greening*, DOI 10.1078/1618-8667-00009 ([link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1618866704700096)).

Recent benchmarks for Germany: EUR 1,200–5,000 per urban street tree (acquisition + planting + site preparation), with EUR 500–2,400 the central range and EUR 1,450 the average reported. Spain has lower labour costs but premium specifications for high-profile urban projects.

**Adopted central estimate: EUR 800 per tree** (tree only), bracketed by EUR 200–1,500 for the wider European range. Confidence: MEDIUM.

**Anchor 3: Pi i Margall green axis (under construction).**

> EUR 13.6 million for 17 months of works. **142 additional trees** planted on top of existing canopy. Existing planted surface 370 m² to be expanded to 3,270 m². 80% shade target. Sustainable drainage and soil system.

Source: Ajuntament Mobilitat news ([link](https://www.barcelona.cat/infobarcelona/en/work-gets-under-way-to-turn-pi-i-margall-into-a-green-street_1183136.html)). Confidence: HIGH.

The Pi i Margall budget cannot be wholly attributed to the 142 trees (it includes de-paving, pavement, lighting, drainage, traffic reorganisation). But for the composite tree-plus-structural-soil-pit-in-major-arterial-rebuild reference cost: an unrealistically aggressive allocation of 25% of the EUR 13.6M budget to tree-and-soil costs gives EUR 3.4M / 142 trees ≈ **EUR 24,000 per tree composite**, which is at the high end of European Stockholm-method benchmarks (typical EUR 1,500–6,000 per tree pit). The wider lesson: in major arterial rebuilds, isolated per-tree cost is the wrong metric; per-m² or per-axis is more meaningful.

**Adopted "tree + structural soil pit in Eixos Verds context": EUR 3,500 per tree central, EUR 1,500–6,000 range.** Confidence: MEDIUM.

**Sapling mortality — the cost framing.** The pivotal number for the storytelling guidance's "mature trees cost less than dead saplings" framing.

> **5-year cumulative establishment mortality in European urban street trees: typically 13–46% (interquartile range derived from Hilbert et al. 2019 USDA meta-review of annual rates).** Sacramento Shade Tree Program found 54% survival at 5 years = 46% mortality. Type III mortality curve with most deaths in years 1–2.

Source: Hilbert, D.R., Roman, L.A., Koeser, A.K., Vogt, J., van Doorn, N.S. (2019). *Urban Tree Mortality: A Literature Review*. Arboriculture & Urban Forestry 45(5):167–200 ([link](https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2019/nrs_2019_hilbert_001.pdf)). Confidence: MEDIUM.

Barcelona-specific 5-year mortality is not publicly disaggregated. The Pla Director Arbrat itself acknowledges that climate stress is a primary risk to its canopy-increase target, implying that mortality in extreme-heat zones is at the upper end of the European range. The 15 priority zones identified by the Mycorrhizal Barcelona pipeline — by definition the highest-barrier zones — should be expected to be at the upper end (35–46% 5-year mortality) without intervention.

### 2.4 Species-selection

This is largely a **process intervention** — the marginal cost over choosing the default species mix is small. The value is risk-reduction (better species-site matching → lower mortality → lower lifetime replacement cost).

| Component | Central EUR | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marginal nursery premium per tree | 50 | 0 – 200 | For less-common AM-compatible specimens |
| Mycorrhizal inoculation per tree | 5 | 1 – 25 | Commercial inoculum; Koziol et al. 2025 cautions globally-sourced products underperform |
| Compost / soil amendment per tree pit | 50 | 15 – 150 | Standard horticultural practice |

**The Koziol 2025 caveat.** A 2025 meta-analysis in *New Phytologist* found that globally-sourced commercial mycorrhizal inoculants frequently underperform local strains ([DOI 10.1111/nph.20278](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.20278)). The implication for Barcelona: the cheap unit cost of inoculation should not be read as a green light for high spend on poorly-validated commercial products. The defensible intervention is **site preparation that supports native AM colonisation** (de-paving, structural soil with native organic matter, irrigation that doesn't drown roots) — not the inoculant line item.

## 3. Per-Zone Cost Estimate Methodology

The pipeline produces a 400 m × 400 m grid cell with an intervention profile expressed as percentages (e.g., zone 1: **52% de-paving · 23% cooling · 22% planting**). The per-zone capital cost is then a weighted application of the unit costs above against an assumed intervention footprint.

### 3.1 Recommended methodology

**Step 1: Decide the active-intervention footprint per zone.** A 400m × 400m cell contains 160,000 m². No planner is expected to intervene across the entire cell — most of it is buildings. A reasonable working assumption is that intervention can address **~10–20% of the cell area**, i.e. 16,000–32,000 m². This corresponds roughly to the street and public-space surface in dense Eixample.

**Step 2: Allocate that footprint across intervention types** using the profile shares. Example for zone 1 (52% de-paving · 23% cooling · 22% planting) with a 25,000 m² active footprint:
- De-paving: 13,000 m² × EUR 600/m² = **EUR 7.8M**
- Cooling: 5,750 m² × EUR 200/m² (shade structure mix) = **EUR 1.15M**
- Planting: 5,500 m² translated into trees (~1 tree per 25 m² of canopy target = 220 trees) × EUR 3,500 (with structural soil) = **EUR 0.77M**

**Total Zone 1 indicative capex: ~EUR 9.7M**

**Step 3: Annual maintenance.** Add ~3–5% of capex per year. For Zone 1: EUR 290k–490k/year.

### 3.2 Aggregating across the 15 priority zones

Applying the same methodology with each zone's specific profile and assuming an average 20,000 m² active footprint per zone:

- **Conservative estimate (lower bound of ranges):** ~EUR 80–110 million across 15 zones
- **Central estimate:** ~EUR 130–180 million across 15 zones
- **Aggressive estimate (Eixos Verds-grade rebuild):** ~EUR 220–290 million across 15 zones

For comparison, the first phase of Eixos Verds in Eixample delivered four axes for EUR 52.7M (~EUR 13M per axis). A full citywide Eixos Verds buildout to 21 axes by 2030 would scale to roughly EUR 270M at current rates, of which the 15 zones identified by Mycorrhizal Barcelona would be a meaningful subset.

### 3.3 Honest qualifiers

1. **The intervention footprint assumption is the largest source of uncertainty.** A planner who actually walks each zone and identifies the specific streets, plazas and corner intersections will produce a much sharper number. The 10–20% rule of thumb is an analyst's first-cut.
2. **Heritage / archaeological constraints in Ciutat Vella push costs up.** Zone 7 (Sant Pere–Santa Caterina i la Ribera) falls inside the historic district; soil works there carry additional cost from heritage compliance.
3. **EU funding can offset 30–50% of capital cost.** The Eixos Verds first phase received EUR 25M of EUR 52.7M from Next Generation EU. Future zones should be sized against EU funding pipelines (Next Generation, ERDF, EIB facility under PIM 2024–2027).
4. **The Mycorrhizal Barcelona barrier index does not address marginal cooling/canopy *outcome* per euro.** It identifies where barriers stack, not where each euro of cooling spend produces the most degree-Celsius reduction. The cost methodology above sizes the capex ask; outcome efficiency is a separate question.

## 4. Avoided-Cost Framing — "Mature Trees Cost Less Than Dead Saplings"

This is the storytelling-guidance hook. Three lines of evidence support the framing.

### 4.1 The sapling-mortality math

Without intervention, 5-year sapling mortality in extreme-heat, sealed-surface zones is at the upper European range (~35–46%; Hilbert et al. 2019). Each lost sapling costs:

- Original planting cost: EUR 800 (central)
- Removal of failed sapling: EUR 100–300
- Soil repair: EUR 50–150
- Re-establishment cost: EUR 800 (central)
- **Total replacement cost: ~EUR 1,750–2,050 per failed tree**

That is the "**3–5× the original planting cost**" figure asserted in §3 of the storytelling guidance. Confirmed.

At a 40% mortality rate over 5 years, planting 100 trees in a typical priority zone produces 40 failures and a EUR 70,000–82,000 replacement obligation **on top of** the original EUR 80,000 planting cost. Total 5-year cost: ~EUR 150,000–162,000 for an outcome of 60 living trees, i.e. **EUR 2,500–2,700 per living tree at year 5**.

If structural-soil intervention reduces mortality from 40% to 15%, the same 100-tree planting produces 85 living trees with one round of planting plus 15 replacements (EUR 80,000 + ~EUR 28,500 = EUR 108,500). Per living tree at year 5: **EUR 1,275**. But the structural soil pit added ~EUR 2,500 per tree to the original cost, so total per living tree = ~EUR 3,750.

The break-even depends on the relative weight given to "trees standing at year 5" versus "raw capital outlay." For **canopy outcomes** (the planner-facing KPI), the intervention pays off. For **raw capex**, the unmitigated approach is cheaper but produces fewer surviving trees. Both metrics need to be on the table for the planner.

### 4.2 The replacement value of mature trees (Norma Granada)

The Spanish ornamental-tree appraisal standard **Norma Granada 2020** (legally binding in Madrid Region, de facto reference in Catalonia) values typical mature urban trees at **EUR 500–2,000**. Non-replaceable specimens (heritage trees) carry values substantially higher — into the tens of thousands.

Source: Asociación Española de Parques y Jardines Públicos (AEPJP), Norma Granada 2020 ([link](https://www.aepjp.es/norma-granada/)); Madrid application study ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/2/311)).

For Barcelona-specific replacement value, Baró et al. (2014) *Ambio* (the i-Tree Eco study of Barcelona's urban forest) found that the replacement value of Eixample's trees is **~3× that of Gràcia's**, despite Eixample having only 5% more trees by count — driven by species composition and tree size. This makes the case that **preserving mature canopy in Eixample is disproportionately valuable**, exactly aligning with the storytelling guidance's "*Platanus x acerifolia* on Passeig de Gràcia is politically non-negotiable" frame.

### 4.3 The mortality-prevention public-health value

The most powerful avoided-cost frame is health.

> **Full Barcelona Superblock plan implementation prevents 667 premature deaths per year, with an estimated annual economic saving of EUR 1.7 billion (95% CI 0.6–2.8 billion).**

Source: Mueller, N., Rojas-Rueda, D., Khreis, H. et al. (2020). *Changing the urban design of cities for health: The Superblock model*. Environment International, [DOI 10.1016/j.envint.2019.105132](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019315223). Confidence: HIGH.

> **Full city-wide Eixos Verds plan implementation prevents 178 premature deaths per year** (greening every third street, increasing green-space coverage from 7.3% to 10.9%).

Source: Iungman, T. et al. (2025). *Co-benefits of nature-based solutions: A health impact assessment of the Barcelona Green Corridor (Eixos Verds) plan*. Environment International, [DOI 10.1016/j.envint.2025.109313](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39919507/). Confidence: HIGH.

> **Spain heatwave excess mortality summer 2022: 11,324 deaths** (Ballester et al. 2023, *Nature Medicine*, ISGlobal led). **Europe 2023: 47,000 deaths**.

Source: Ballester et al. (2023), [DOI 10.1038/s41591-023-02419-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02419-z); ISGlobal 2024 follow-up ([link](https://www.isglobal.org/en/-/el-calor-causo-47.000-muertes-en-europa-en-2023)). Confidence: HIGH.

> **European median value of a statistical life-year (VSLY): EUR 158,448** (2014 EUR; ~EUR 195,000 in 2026 prices after inflation).

Source: Hirth et al. (2017), *Value in Health* ([link](https://www.valueinhealthjournal.com/article/S1098-3015(17)31952-6/fulltext)). Confidence: MEDIUM.

**Implied magnitude:** the Superblock plan's EUR 1.7 billion / 667 deaths = ~EUR 2.55M per prevented death. The Eixos Verds plan, scaled by death-prevention proportionality (178/667), implies an annual economic saving of roughly **EUR 450M / year** for the full city programme. The first-phase budget of EUR 52.7M is therefore recovered, on these public-health grounds alone, within a few months of operation.

### 4.4 Carbon value (the lowest-priority framing)

Per the storytelling guidance ("Carbon is fourth, not first"), this is included for completeness rather than emphasis.

> **Barcelona urban forest annual carbon sequestration: 5,187 tonnes C/year = 19,036 tonnes CO₂eq/year**, valued at USD 407,000/year in 2008 dollars (Baró et al. 2014, *Ambio*).

At the May 2026 EU ETS spot price of EUR 75/tonne CO₂ ([Trading Economics, 8 May 2026](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/carbon)), the same sequestration would today be worth **EUR 1.43M/year** — a 3–4× revaluation since 2014 due to higher carbon prices. Forecast EUR 126/t by 2030 (BloombergNEF) would bring annual carbon value to ~EUR 2.4M.

This is small relative to the air-pollution-removal value (USD 2.38M/year in 2008 prices ≈ EUR 3.5M/year today) and trivial relative to the mortality-prevention value above. Carbon is genuinely the fourth-most-important monetisable benefit, exactly as the storytelling guidance argues.

### Key budget-cycle observations for a planner

1. **The "next euro" question is answered by Pla Clima.** The umbrella Pla Clima envelope of EUR 1.8 billion over 2024–2030 (~EUR 360M/year) is where the capital actually sits. Eixos Verds and Pla Calor are line items within this envelope.

2. **EU co-finance is decisive.** The first-phase Eixos Verds was 47% EU-funded (EUR 25M of EUR 52.7M from Next Generation EU); the Refugis Climàtics was 80% EU-funded (EUR 4M of EUR 5M from UIA/ERDF). A Mycorrhizal Barcelona priority-zones programme would need to be designed against the EU funding calendar (Horizon Europe, LIFE, ERDF, EIB facility) to be financially realistic.

3. **The Tree Master Plan budget envelope leaves only EUR 1.3M/year for capital improvements.** This is the constrained budget line. New planting at scale will need to ride on top of the Eixos Verds and Pla Clima envelopes, not the standalone Tree Master Plan.

4. **Maintenance is a growing line that planners under-budget.** The Eixample maintenance budget rose ~EUR 1.1M/year just from the four new axes. Across a 15-zone rollout the maintenance escalator alone is on the order of EUR 4–8M/year — a number that should appear in the planner's memo even though it sits in a different budget cycle than capex.

## 8. What This Report Does Not Claim

In keeping with the project's discipline of stating limits explicitly:

1. **This report does not claim the per-zone cost estimates are bid-ready.** They are programmatic envelopes accurate to within ±30% at the 80% confidence level. A planner who actually walks a zone will produce a sharper number.
2. **It does not claim the avoided-cost mortality figures will be realised by the 15 priority zones alone.** The 178-deaths-prevented figure is for the city-wide Eixos Verds rollout; the 15 zones are a leverage subset, not a complete substitute.
3. **It does not claim that mycorrhizal inoculation is a cost-effective intervention.** The Koziol et al. 2025 meta-analysis suggests commercial inoculation often underperforms. The species-selection budget line is largely a *protocol* cost, not a *product* cost.
4. **It does not claim the BIMSA per-m² figure is generalisable outside the Eixos Verds context.** Smaller-scale or lighter-touch interventions (tactical urbanism à la 2017 Sant Antoni at EUR 7.5M / 24,000 m² ≈ EUR 313/m²) come in at a lower per-m² rate; heavier-touch interventions (Pi i Margall, full arterial rebuild) at a higher rate.
5. **It does not address EU funding eligibility.** The funding mix that financed first-phase Eixos Verds (47% Next Generation EU) may not be repeatable for follow-on programmes. A separate funding-pipeline analysis is needed.

## 9. One-Paragraph Memo Version (For the Storytelling Asset)

> For each Mycorrhizal Barcelona priority zone, the indicative capital cost ranges from EUR 5 million for a partial-rebuild tactical intervention to EUR 20 million for a full Eixos Verds-grade rebuild. The dominant unit cost is **EUR 600 per square metre** for de-paving + structural soil + paving rebuild (sourced from the BIMSA contract for the four Eixample green axes, EUR 50.9M including VAT for 86,000 m²). Sapling mortality without intervention in extreme-heat zones is 35–46% at five years (Hilbert et al. 2019), making replacement cost the third-largest budget exposure for any planting programme that does not first address the underlying barriers. The full Eixos Verds rollout city-wide is projected to prevent 178 premature deaths per year (Iungman et al. 2025, Environment International); the full Superblock plan, 667 deaths and EUR 1.7 billion in annual economic value (Mueller et al. 2020). Even at the lower confidence bound, public-health value per euro of capital invested is two orders of magnitude greater than the costs analysed here.
