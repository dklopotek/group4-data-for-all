# Five Whys Analysis

## Starting request

"We want to map mycorrhizal network fragmentation in Barcelona to guide urban greening."

## Why #1

**Why map mycorrhizal network fragmentation?**

Because belowground fungal networks connect trees, share resources, and support soil health — and urban infrastructure (pavement, compaction, heat) breaks those connections. If we can map where the network is most broken, we can target restoration.

## Why #2

**Why does targeting belowground network restoration matter?**

Because Barcelona is spending significant capital on green infrastructure (Superilla, Eixos Verds) and that spending should address the full ecological system — not just visible tree canopy but the invisible fungal networks that keep trees alive and soil functioning. Without the belowground component, green infrastructure is structurally incomplete.

## Why #3

**Why is belowground ecological completeness important for Barcelona's green-infrastructure spending?**

Because the stated goals of those programmes include climate resilience, biodiversity, and long-term urban sustainability. Trees planted in compacted, sealed, fungally-depleted soil have higher mortality, lower growth, and deliver less cooling and carbon storage. If the belowground dimension is ignored, the programmes underperform against their own objectives.

## Why #4

**Why do the programmes currently ignore the belowground dimension?**

Because it is invisible. Soil fungi cannot be seen from a planning office. There is no existing data product that translates belowground ecological science into a format planners can use — a map with scores, priorities, and budget-line references.

## Why #5

**Why does no such data product exist?**

Because the science-to-policy translation chain for soil ecology is broken: the scientists measure fungal communities (via DNA metabarcoding, which is sparse in Iberia), the planners allocate capital via general criteria (traffic, visibility, politics), and nobody has built the intermediary layer — a pragmatic, data-driven prioritization tool that uses *measurable proxies* for belowground barriers (sealed surface, heat, canopy, host compatibility) to route capital to the zones where intervention has highest expected leverage.

## Root decision

**The root need is not a fungal network map. It is a decision-support tool that closes the science-to-policy translation gap for belowground ecology in urban capital allocation.**

## Implication for decision statement

The Five Whys confirms the v2 reframe (from "fragmentation map" to "barrier-reduction priority map") is correct. The original v1 framing ("map the network") was a symptom of the science-to-policy gap. The v2 framing ("rank zones by measurable barriers, recommend intervention type, tie to budget line") directly addresses the root cause: planners need a tool that translates belowground ecological considerations into their existing decision framework.

**Decision statement unchanged** — already consistent with the root decision surfaced here.
